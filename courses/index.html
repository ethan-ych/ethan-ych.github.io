Course Reviews

In these reviews, I chose to focus on how each class would fit into my (and maybe your) Computer Science education, rather than giving advice on how to succeed in the course. I think CMU has a unique curriculum in the sense that it exposes you to many fundamental areas of the field, and I hope this guide can help you take advantage of that. It is worth noting that I wrote all these reviews after completing undergrad, so I was able to evaulate these courses with a fuller picture.

This page contains (or at least will contain) every class I took at CMU, not just limited to CS or math. If you don’t see any comment on a particular course, it probably means that I don’t have much to say about it. Lastly, if you have any questions about the reviews, please don’t hesitate to reach out to me! I’d be glad to know someone is reading my reviews.

Spring 2022

12-100 Exploring CEE: Infrastructure and Environment in a Changing World
15-122 Principles of Imperative Computation, Iliano Cervesato

This is a good course in the sense that it builds you a very solid foundation in basic data structures and C programming. However, it was delivered in a rather unexciting way.

15-150 Principles of Functional Programming, Michael Erdmann and Karl Crary

It is not an overstatment to say that this course decided my progression at CMU. Before taking this course, I had never heard of functional programming, but I was instantly captured by its elegance and its unique interpretation of computation. This was the first course at CMU that really challenged me intellectually, but as I got more used to “thinking functionally,” I became more comfortable with the materials.

I ended up TA’d this course twice and was subsequently fed into the “programming language (PL) pipeline” as I took 312 and compilers. Let me take the chance to share my two cents on functional programming (FP): it’s not the solution to everything and I don’t believe we should ditch imperative and embrace functional. FP however excels in certain areas like safety and expressiveness (strong type systems/higher order functions), so perhaps a mixture of the two is the answer.

Fall 2021

18-100 Introduction to Electrical and Computer Engineering, Jimmy Zhu

I started out as an ECE major when I entered CMU which is why I took this class. Since I then switched majors, I didn’t really get to use anything I learned from this class. However, it ended up completing the “missing piece” in my CS education: CMU CS doesn’t require their students to take any hardware class, so if it wasn’t for this class, I likely wouldn’t know how a digital computer functions down to transitors level.

21-122 Integration and Approximation, Lihan Wang

I had AP credit for Calc 2 so I didn’t need to take this class. I only took it because my freshman CIT advisor wrote to us in an email and I quote

A word on calculus: it is the fundamental core to engineering (you could say it’s integral to engineering…womp womp).

Haha… Throughout the class I didn’t feel like I needed a refresher on the content. Therefore I would suggest not repeating a class you have credit for unless you have quite literally forgot everything.

21-127 Concepts of Mathematics, Clive Newstead

My favorite class this semster. It introduces all the basic ideas in college-level math and teaches you how to write proofs. The class showed me a whole new world of mathematics and I absolutely loved it. This was the point where I decided to pursue a minor in math.
